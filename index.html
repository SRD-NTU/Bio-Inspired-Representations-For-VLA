01 / THE IDEA

Abstract

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have emerged as a powerful paradigm for language-conditioned robotic manipulation. However, standard VLA pipelines typically process visual observations with spatially uniform fidelity, allocating equal resources to regions regardless of their relevance to the ongoing action. In contrast, human perception continuously adapts where visual detail is allocated according to the demands of interaction. Inspired by this principle, we propose CORTAct, a bio-inspired, training-free framework that closes the perception-action loop by using feedback from a frozen VLA to adapt the visual representation presented to subsequent policy queries. Rather than treating visual allocation independently of action generation, CORTAct derives spatial interaction relevance from the policy's own action-conditioned signals. It then temporally aggregates and predicts this relevance to perform non-uniform sampling under a fixed visual budget toward regions likely to support upcoming interactions. To realize this adaptive allocation, CORTAct employs a Human-like Adaptive Visual Representation (AdVisR) module that redistributes spatial resolution using a multi-focus Möbius-parameterized transformation. We extensively evaluate CORTAct across multiple VLA backbones, visual allocation baselines, and embodied manipulation benchmarks. We also explore two complementary non-uniform representations using CORTAct to study the respective roles of local action-relevant detail and global scene context. Our results show that budgeted visual representations improve VLA performance by up to +20\
